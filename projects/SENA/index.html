Why does this work?

One representation

The tissue image is encoded once; every output reads from the same shared representation.

Semantics on demand

Nucleus type benefits from richer semantic features. They are added at inference, by the same trained model, only where requested.

Shape on demand

Exact boundaries come from a separate local refiner, added only when contours matter.

PanNuke quantitative evaluation

SENA exposes four inference modes from one trained system. Eff provides the lowest-compute operating point; Cls activates direct pathology-aware semantic information; Seg adds local spatial refinement; Full activates both.

Tumour-region readout (PAIP2020)

Classified nuclei are aggregated into a tissue-level tumour region without a separate tumour-segmentation model: 100-µm bins with a Neoplastic majority form the core, and nearby bins dominated by connective or dead nuclei are added within 500 µm. The frozen system and the fixed construction were applied to 44 PAIP2020 slides with pathologist tumour outlines, without fine-tuning or threshold tuning.

Accuracy and computational cost

SENA does not optimise for a single fixed operating point. Its configurable inference paths span several accuracy–cost operating points; in this benchmark they expand the observed accuracy–runtime Pareto frontier, while computation can be allocated according to task requirements: Eff is the inexpensive base analysis, Cls adds semantic computation, Seg adds local spatial refinement, and Full adds both. All four are inference modes of one frozen package; nothing is retrained between them.

Observed accuracy–runtime frontier

All 6 comparison methods are Pareto-dominated by at least one SENA operating point (matched or beaten on both runtime and avgPQ). Frontier of the comparison methods alone: NuLite-T, CellViT-H, PromptNucSeg-H, NuHTC. Frontier after adding SENA: Eff, Cls, Seg and Full — all four operating points of the one package, and no comparison method.

Secondary summary — relative hypervolume (HV) gain: +59.1% under the fixed normalisation below (HV 0.417 for the comparison methods, 0.664 with SENA). The magnitude is reference-dependent (+33% to +155% across the tested sensitivity range), whereas the positive gain and Pareto-front composition are unchanged. Normalisation: two objectives, avgPQ (maximise) and runtime (minimise), u = (60 − runtime)/60 and v = (avgPQ − 35)/(50 − 35); HV is the area of the union of the rectangles [0, u] × [0, v]; relative gain = (HV with SENA − HV without)/HV without. Sensitivity: reference runtime 55–90 min, reference avgPQ 30–39.5, and a log-runtime variant. Runtime and avgPQ come from different benchmarks; HV summarises the frontier as a whole and is not a per-method score. †Checkpoints are not matched for every comparison method: avgPQ values of the comparison methods are those reported in the original publications (three-fold), whereas the whole-slide runs used the CellViT-SAM-H public ×40 checkpoint, the released NuHTC pannuke.pth, the official PromptNucSeg split-213 weights, the official HoVer-Net PanNuke checkpoint and the NuLite PanNuke weights (trained on all of PanNuke, so no held-out score exists for that checkpoint); SENA avgPQ is the three-split mean of its training procedure and the whole-slide runs used the release package (v1.0.1).
