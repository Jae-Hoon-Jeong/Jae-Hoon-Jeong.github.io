Why does this work?

One representation

The tissue image is encoded once; every output reads from the same shared representation.

Semantics on demand

Nucleus type benefits from richer semantic features. They are added at inference, by the same trained model, only where requested.

Shape on demand

Exact boundaries come from a separate local refiner, added only when contours matter.

PanNuke quantitative evaluation

SENA exposes four inference modes from one trained system. Eff provides the lowest-compute operating point; Cls activates direct pathology-aware semantic information; Seg adds local spatial refinement; Full activates both.

Classified nuclei are aggregated into a tissue-level tumour region without a separate tumour-segmentation model: 100-µm bins with a Neoplastic majority form the core, and nearby bins dominated by connective or dead nuclei are added within 500 µm. The frozen system and the fixed construction were applied to 44 PAIP2020 slides with pathologist tumour outlines, without fine-tuning or threshold tuning.

These results establish the tissue-level readout; we next asked which cellular information drives it and whether that signal extends further downstream (Cross-scale).

From cellular identity to tissue-level pathology

High nuclei-analysis accuracy is only useful pathologically if the resulting cellular representation stays informative beyond individual nuclei. Tumour tissue is not defined simply by where nuclei are dense: cellular identity and composition are central cues in pathological interpretation. We therefore asked whether pathology-informed cellular identity changes the tissue-level interpretation when everything else is held fixed, and how far that signal travels downstream.

Same nuclei, different identity

Held fixedthe exact same nuclei, their number, positions and shapes, and the same tumour-region aggregation rule
Changedonly the cell-type label of each nucleus (Eff identity → Cls identity)

On the 44 PAIP2020 slides, changing cellular identity alone improved the tissue-level tumour map: tumour Dice +0.069 [+0.050, +0.089], 39 better / 5 worse of 44 slides. This isolates the effect of the semantic representation on cellular identity; it does not show that surrounding tissue context by itself caused the improvement.

better pathology-informed identity → fewer non-neoplastic cells labelled neoplastic outside the tumour → fewer tissue-level false-positive regions

Not simply where the cells are

Where are the cells?position + densityDice 0.678
What cells are they?identity + compositionDice 0.837

Could the tumour map simply reflect where nuclei are dense? A control that keeps every SENA detection but discards all class information reached a lower Dice (+0.159 [+0.130, +0.188] in favour of identity, 43 better / 1 worse of 44 slides). When different nuclei models produce different tumour maps, the difference was explained by cellular composition (+0.049 [+0.033, +0.066]) rather than by detection support (+0.0002 [−0.0020, +0.0020]). Identity and composition, rather than nuclei abundance alone, carry the relevant tissue-level information.

These mechanistic analyses compare maps only within their frozen evaluation protocol (canonical tumour-map scorer; Dice 0.837 vs 0.678 above). The comparison table in the previous section uses the common benchmark scorer, which additionally clips every method to the release tissue mask at 25 µm; this is why SENA Cls reads 0.834 there.

Independent cell and tissue annotations

PAIP2020 compares tumour maps with pathologist outlines. OCELOT adds an independent test with both cell-level and tissue-level ground truth (298 slides, 654 regions, 111,613 annotated tumour and background cells). Does better cellular identity appear as better cell-level classification, and does the gain reach the tissue-level readout?

Cellular identity improved clearly. The effect propagated to tissue-level tumour-cell enrichment, although the tissue-level effect was modest and not individually significant within any single organ.

How far does the signal propagate downstream?

If cell-derived tumour maps better identify biologically meaningful tumour tissue, they might also improve the selection of regions for spatial or molecular downstream analyses. We tested this in a public colorectal spatial-transcriptomics cohort (6 patients).

Small-cohort supportive observation

Strict high-purity tumour readout

A strict readout taken directly from SENA Cls nuclear classifications (spots with > 90% predicted neoplastic nuclei, the pathology strict-tumour definition) matched pathologist strict-tumour spots better than the best external tumour-region map (VISTA-PATH): patient-median Dice 0.628 vs 0.531, higher in 5 of 6 patients. Its RNA profile was closer to the strict-tumour reference in all 6 patients (median distance 0.0093 vs 0.0181).

A separate, more specific readout, not the tumour-region map: higher precision at the cost of lower recall. The canonical tumour region itself was not closer than VISTA-PATH. Six patients only.

Superiority not established

Matched true-tumour coverage

When the amount of true tumour selected was matched across methods, SENA-selected regions were not robustly more representative of the tumour's molecular programmes. Cell-derived neoplastic scores were associated with malignant programmes, but the advantage was not consistently specific to SENA and did not hold after adjusting for purity.

Current evidence supports transfer from cellular identity to tissue-level tumour mapping, but does not yet establish that SENA selects molecularly superior tumour subregions at matched true-tumour coverage.

What do we learn across scales?

  1. Better cellular identity changes tissue-level tumour mapping even when cellular geometry is fixed.
  2. The gain is not explained by cell density or detection support alone; cellular composition carries the key information.
  3. Molecular follow-up is encouraging but currently does not establish SENA-specific downstream superiority.

The strongest current evidence supports a pathology-informed cellular identity → tissue-level tumour mapping link.

Accuracy and computational cost

SENA does not optimise for a single fixed operating point. Its configurable inference paths span several accuracy–cost operating points; in this benchmark they expand the observed accuracy–resource Pareto frontiers, while computation can be allocated according to task requirements: Eff is the inexpensive base analysis, Cls adds semantic computation, Seg adds local spatial refinement, and Full adds both. All four are inference modes of one frozen package; nothing is retrained between them.

Accuracy–resource Pareto frontiers

SENA’s accuracy–efficiency advantage is not restricted to runtime. In this benchmark its configurable modes occupy strong Pareto operating points across time, energy and memory constraints.
